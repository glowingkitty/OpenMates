---
title: Application conventions by country
description: What a CV and application letter usually contain in Germany, the USA, the UK and Sweden, and what to leave out.
when_to_use: Writing or reviewing a CV, resume, cover letter or application for a specific country, or when the user asks what is expected there.
---
Conventions follow the country of the employer, not of the applicant. Ask which
country the application is for when it is not clear. A requirement in the job
posting always takes priority over this guidance. For a country not listed here,
research the official employment service or a university careers service first
and say what you found; do not guess.

Personal details such as a photo, date of birth, nationality or marital status are
the user's choice everywhere. Say what is customary, say that leaving them out is
acceptable, and never press for them. This is not legal advice.

Germany
- Name: "Lebenslauf". Tabular form: dates on the left, entries on the right,
  grouped by topic, newest first within each group. Usually no more than two pages.
- Build the tabular look with tab stops or a simple two-column arrangement that
  still reads correctly line by line; avoid nested tables and text boxes.
- A photo is customary but voluntary. Date and place of birth, nationality and
  marital status are not required; official guidance says to leave them out when
  in doubt. Religion is not included.
- Place, date and signature at the end are traditional and optional for digital
  applications.
- A one-page application letter ("Anschreiben") is normally expected unless the
  posting says otherwise. Certificates and employer references ("Zeugnisse") are
  usually attached, commonly combined into one PDF.
- Austria and Switzerland are similar. In Switzerland employer references carry
  particular weight. Confirm details for the specific posting.

United States
- Name: "resume". One to two pages. "CV" means a longer academic or medical record.
- No photo, age, date of birth, marital status, nationality or religion.
- City and state are enough; a full street address is not needed.
- Use the exact requirement terms from the posting where they are true. Dates with
  month and four-digit year.
- A cover letter is often optional. Write one when the posting asks, or when it
  adds something the resume cannot.

United Kingdom
- Name: "CV". Commonly up to two pages.
- Sections: contact details, a short introduction of a few lines, work history
  newest first, education, optional references.
- Do not include age, date of birth, marital status or nationality. A photo is
  not expected.
- Do not put referees' contact details on the CV.

Sweden
- CV of one to two pages focused on experience relevant to the job. A photo is
  optional.
- A personal letter ("personligt brev") of at most one page is common. It adds to
  the CV and does not repeat it.
- Applications are normally in Swedish unless the posting is in English or the
  working language is English.
- Sending both documents as PDF with clear file names is usual when emailing.

Language
- Write the application in the language of the job posting unless the user says
  otherwise. Use that language's usual section headings.

Europass
- The official EU format is accepted for cross-border and public-sector
  applications. Offer it only when the user or the posting asks for it; many
  private employers prefer an individually written CV.
