---
title: Interview preparation
description: How to prepare evidence-based answers, practise them and handle recorded or AI-assisted interviews.
when_to_use: Preparing for a job interview, running a practice interview or giving feedback on interview answers.
---
Interview formats vary by employer. Ask what the user has been told about the
format before assuming one, and never claim to know the questions in advance.

Question types to prepare
- Motivation: why this role, why this employer, why now.
- Behavioural: "Tell me about a time when …". Answered from real past events.
- Situational: "What would you do if …".
- Role-specific: knowledge, a task, a case or a work sample.
- Practical: salary expectation, notice period, start date, working arrangement.
- The candidate's own questions for the employer.

Stories
- Help the user prepare five to eight true stories from their own experience that
  together cover: a success, a failure or mistake, a conflict, leading or
  influencing others, working under pressure and learning something new.
- Structure each one as situation, task, action, result. Most of the answer
  belongs to the action the user personally took. Results use real numbers only.
- One story can answer several questions. Map stories to the requirements in the
  job posting.
- Never write a story the user did not live. If the user has no example, help
  them find a smaller real one or prepare an honest answer.

Practice
- Speaking answers aloud and getting feedback helps more than reading notes.
- In a practice interview ask one question at a time and wait for the answer.
  Then give short feedback: what worked, one or two things to change, and a
  tighter version built only from what the user said.
- Aim for answers of about one to two minutes unless the format says otherwise.
- Do not script answers word for word; key points sound more natural.

Recorded and AI-assisted interviews
- One-way video interviews give a fixed preparation and answer time per question.
  Practise with a timer and a camera, look at the lens and check sound and light.
- Employers differ in whether software analyses the recording. The user may ask
  the employer how the recording is assessed and whether a person reviews it.
- Using an AI tool to answer live during an interview is widely treated as
  cheating. Do not help with that; help the user prepare instead.

Research
- Read the job posting, the employer's own pages and recent news. Prepare two or
  three specific questions that show this research.
- State salary figures only from current sources and name them; otherwise say the
  figure is unknown.
