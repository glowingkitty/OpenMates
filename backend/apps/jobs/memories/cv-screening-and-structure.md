---
title: CV structure and how employers screen applications
description: How applicant tracking software and AI screening read a CV, and the structure that survives both.
when_to_use: Creating, updating, reviewing or tailoring a CV or resume, or answering questions about applicant tracking systems and AI screening.
---
Use this as working guidance, not as guaranteed rules. Screening software differs
between employers and vendors rarely publish how they parse. Say so when asked for
certainty, and never promise that a CV will "pass" a system.

What is known and what is not
- Applicant tracking software mainly stores applications, extracts their text into
  fields and lets recruiters search, filter and rank. A badly parsed CV is more
  often overlooked than automatically rejected.
- The claim that most CVs are rejected by software before a human sees them has no
  reliable source. There is no universal "ATS score"; scores shown by CV-builder
  sites are their own invention. Do not quote such figures.
- More employers now use AI models to summarise and rank applications. These match
  meaning, not only exact words, so specific and verifiable statements work better
  than keyword lists.
- Hidden text, white-on-white keywords and instructions aimed at an AI screener do
  not help and are increasingly detected and treated as deception. Never add them,
  even when asked; explain why instead.
- Recruiters report many near-identical, generic AI-written applications. Concrete
  facts in the applicant's own words are what set an application apart.

Structure that reads reliably
- One column, top to bottom. No text boxes, no layout tables, no sidebars.
- Name and contact details in the document body, not in the header or footer.
- Plain section headings a recruiter and a parser both expect in the CV's language,
  for example "Work experience", "Education", "Skills", "Languages".
- Work history newest first: job title, employer, place, start and end date, then
  two to five bullets.
- One consistent date format with a four-digit year, for example "03/2021 – 08/2024".
  Use "present" for a current job. Never leave an end date out.
- Real text only. No skill bars, icons, logos or images that carry information.
- A common font at 10 to 12 points. Real bullet lists.
- Links written out as visible text as well as linked.
- Usually one to two pages. Longer only where the field expects it, such as
  academic CVs.

Content
- Each bullet says what the person did, at what scale and with what result. Use a
  number only when the user supplied it. Never invent or estimate figures, titles,
  dates, employers, degrees or skills.
- Take role terms and required skills from the target job posting and use the same
  wording where it is true for this person. Do not repeat keywords for their own
  sake and do not add skills the person does not have.
- A short profile at the top is optional: two or three factual lines, no adjectives
  without evidence.
- Name gaps briefly and neutrally with year ranges, for example parental leave,
  study, job search or caring for a relative.
- Leave out "references available on request", outdated or irrelevant early jobs
  and generic buzzwords.

File
- A Word document or a text-based PDF both work in current systems. Follow the
  format the posting asks for. A scanned or image-only PDF cannot be read.
- Use a clear file name such as "Firstname_Lastname_CV".

Regional conventions differ on photo, personal details, length and the letter.
Load the regional conventions memory before deciding those points.
