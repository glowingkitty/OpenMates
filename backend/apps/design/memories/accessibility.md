---
title: Accessibility best practices
description: Interfaces that remain perceivable, operable and understandable across users and input methods.
when_to_use: Designing or reviewing interactive UI, forms, visual states, hierarchy or motion.
---
Apply these practices in the target platform. WCAG governs web content; native
interfaces also need their platform guidance. Formal conformance targets and
required exceptions belong in Specifications. Loading a guide does not prove compliance.

- Use meaningful hierarchy, persistent labels and logical reading and focus order.
  Prefer semantic controls that convey their role and state.
- Check text and control contrast across applicable states and themes. Pair color
  with another cue when conveying meaning, status or an error.
- Preserve keyboard reachability, visible focus and predictable exits from menus
  and dialogs. Keep focus transitions connected to the user's action.
- Provide adequate pointer and touch target size or separation using the
  applicable platform and product requirements.
- Label controls clearly and explain requirements and errors with useful
  correction guidance. Do not depend solely on a disappearing placeholder.
- Communicate loading, success and failure accessibly without unnecessarily
  moving focus or interrupting unrelated work.
- Preserve usability under enlarged text, spacing changes, narrow viewports and
  orientation changes. Avoid clipping essential labels and actions.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences and preserve essential meaning without
  animation. WCAG guidance on interaction-triggered animation includes AAA criteria;
  do not present every practice here as an AA obligation.
- Review representative flows with keyboard, zoom, text changes, assistive
  technology and applicable platform accessibility settings.

These practices complement required product behavior and mandatory protocols;
they do not waive either.
